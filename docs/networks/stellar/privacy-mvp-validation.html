Kletia + Stellar Privacy — Architecture Validation

Date: 21 August 2026. Status: validated product direction, not shipped privacy functionality.

Verdict: pivot from mandatory privacy transit to a disclosure-aware privacy control plane and confidential treasury. A four-week Stellar Testnet prototype is credible. Sending every Base-to-Arbitrum route through Stellar is not: a short, same-amount round trip is publicly linkable and adds cost and failure surfaces without meaningful privacy.

The product boundary

Kletia should optimize three independent outcomes: economic result, execution risk, and disclosure. Stellar is used continuously to verify private-policy commitments and, when the user chooses, to hold capital inside a confidential treasury. Funds enter Stellar only when that creates a measurable privacy benefit; otherwise Base and Arbitrum execution remains direct.

Example: Base ETH to an Arbitrum lending position

  1. Base ETH is swapped to native Base USDC. The swap and output are public.
  2. A direct route can bridge to Arbitrum and supply to the selected protocol. It is the default when privacy is not requested.
  3. If only the user's strategy or private cap must be hidden, the exact policy stays on the device. Stellar verifies a proof of policy compliance; the funds still take the direct route.
  4. If the user already holds capital in the Stellar confidential treasury, Kletia can reveal and withdraw only the required amount. The remaining treasury balance and internal allocations stay confidential; the EVM exit and lending position become public.
  5. A one-user, immediate Base → Stellar → Arbitrum round trip must never be labelled private. Its amount and timing are easy to correlate.

Current network truth

Circle CCTP V2 now supports Base Mainnet, Stellar Mainnet and Arbitrum One, so public native-USDC transport is technically available. Stellar inbound transfers require the official CctpForwarder; Stellar does not support Circle's destination Forwarding Service, so Kletia needs an XLM-funded relayer for mint_and_forward. CCTP amount fields use six decimals while Stellar USDC uses seven. None of this transport is private or globally atomic.

Real-value privacy remains blocked: OpenZeppelin Confidential Tokens are an unaudited Testnet developer preview, and Nethermind Stellar Private Payments is a work-in-progress, unaudited research implementation. Mainnet CCTP availability does not make these privacy contracts safe for mainnet funds.

Four-week proof of concept

  • An isolated client-side private intent composer that sends only opaque slots to the language model; exact amount, balance, recipient secret, witness and viewing key never enter Kletia API, chat history, logs or analytics.
  • A Stellar Testnet Confidential Token flow for one demo asset: register, deposit, confidential transfer, selective disclosure and withdrawal, with browser-local proving.
  • DisclosurePolicyV1 and DisclosureReceiptV1: list which parties saw each field, where confidentiality begins and ends, and whether the route materially improved privacy.
  • A proof-control-plane demo in which Stellar verifies that a public Base/Arbitrum candidate route satisfies a committed private cap and allowlist, without claiming that Stellar verified external-chain finality.
  • An optional Arc Testnet → Stellar Testnet public CCTP ingress, explicitly showing the transition from public transport into the confidential zone.

Go / no-go gates

  • Network capture confirms no confidential plaintext reaches OpenRouter, the API, telemetry, persisted history or a remote prover.
  • The UI labels every visible address, amount, timing and public ingress/egress boundary; it never says anonymous, untraceable, private bridge or globally atomic.
  • Wrong proof, nullifier, recipient, expiry, verifier, network, wrapper, CCTP domain, forwarder or decimal mapping fails closed.
  • Stellar is selected as a fund route only when a deterministic privacy-gain check shows more than a cosmetic hidden hop.
  • Mainnet privacy remains disabled until the upstream implementation is audited and the Kletia integration has an independent security review.

Positioning

Kletia is a disclosure-aware multichain intent system: Stellar is its confidential treasury and proof-verification domain, while Base, Arbitrum and other networks remain liquidity and execution domains. The defensible behavior is not “Stellar makes every route private.” It is that Kletia can prove a route respects a private policy, explain every disclosure boundary, preserve confidential treasury state, and refuse a route whose claimed privacy cannot actually be delivered.
